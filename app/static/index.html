    How it works

    Four detectors look at the image independently, each in a different way:

    • LightCNN: a small convolutional network trained from scratch on local pixel patterns.
    • EfficientNet-B0: a large ImageNet-pretrained network, fine-tuned (transfer learning).
    • TinyViT: a Vision Transformer that relates all image patches to each other with self-attention.
    • SFHybrid: a new spatial-frequency hybrid that combines convolutional pixel features with the image's Fourier spectrum. AI generators often leave periodic traces there.

    Each model's probability is calibrated with temperature scaling, so that “80 %” means right about 80 % of the time on held-out data. The calibrated scores are combined by a weighted ensemble whose weights were learned on a separate calibration set.

    Reading the explanations

    • Grad-CAM / Grad-CAM++: bright regions influenced the decision most.
    • Integrated Gradients: red pixels push toward “AI-generated”, blue toward “real”.
    • Attention rollout: where the transformer looked.
    • Frequency spectrum: the image's fine-detail pattern, compared with the average real and AI image.

    Limitations (please read)

    • The models were trained only on CIFAKE: 32×32-pixel CIFAR-10 photos versus Stable Diffusion v1.4 images. Larger uploads are shrunk to 32×32, which discards most fine detail.
    • Accuracy drops on images from other generators and after compression, blurring or resizing. See the “Model performance” tab for measured numbers.
    • Heatmaps show what influenced a model. They do not prove an image is fake.
    • Use this tool as decision support only, never as sole evidence.
